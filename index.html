The problem

Who evaluates the evaluators?

Health AI is advancing faster than the infrastructure needed to evaluate its safety, fairness, and clinical readiness. Static benchmarks and aggregate metrics can hide shortcut learning, subgroup failures, domain shift, and breakdowns that only appear in real clinical settings.

The people closest to these failures—clinicians, patients, and communities—are often excluded from evaluation. DOJO addresses this gap by making evaluation continuous, adversarial, participatory, and accountable.

What is DOJO

A gym for health AI.

DOJO provides an evaluation layer between clinical data and deployment. Researchers, clinicians, and evaluation teams can point it at a dataset and one or more models to generate structured evidence about bias, shortcuts, robustness, measurement equity, interoperability, and readiness.

It is modality-agnostic across imaging, tabular EHR data, clinical text, and time-series vitals. It is designed to run locally, support human and agent-assisted workflows, and grow through community-contributed evaluators and challenge sets.

How it works

From declared risk to defensible evidence.

DOJO turns a threat model into controlled challenges, paired observations, independent assurance, and a release decision that cannot average away a critical failure.

Evaluation planes

One system. Three ways to challenge it.

DOJO separates upstream data risks, model behavior, and clinical integration while preserving the evidence that connects them.

01Data PlaneWhat does the dataset make easy to believe?+

Identifies cohort composition changes, documentation inequity, leakage, missingness, and spurious signals that can shape a model before inference begins.

02Model PlaneHow does behavior change under stress?+

Tests robustness to missing or shifted inputs, rare presentations, multimodal contradictions, calibration failures, unsafe confidence, and non-grounded reasoning.

03Clinical Workflow PlaneCan the system function where care happens?+

Evaluates structural and semantic interoperability, pipeline integrity, compute behavior, human-AI interaction, and deployment readiness in context.

01 / Experimental Model

A frozen predictor with a defined task, provenance, and initial robustness profile.

02 / Technical System

Validated adapters, reproducible pipelines, target-like compute, and complete evidence lineage.

03 / Clinical Solution

Local interoperability, workflow fit, human-AI interaction, monitoring, and accountable ownership.

Claims boundary: a functional pilot can demonstrate reproducible challenges, evaluator quality, evidence lineage, and readiness reporting. It cannot by itself establish regulatory approval, universal clinical safety, patient-outcome benefit, or the absence of undiscovered failure modes.
